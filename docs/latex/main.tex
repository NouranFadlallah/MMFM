\begin{abstract}
We report single-modality binary (benign vs.\ malignant) classification
baselines on six public breast-imaging datasets: BUS-BRA
\citep{gomezflores2024busbra}, BUSI \citep{aldhabyani2020busi}, BUSC
\citep{busc2023mendeley,iqbalsharif2023pdfunet}, BrEaST-Lesions USG
\citep{pawlowska2024breast}, mini-MIAS \citep{suckling1994mias}, and
BreastDM \citep{zhao2023breastdm}. They span ultrasound, mammography, and
DCE-MRI. Every dataset uses a pretrained ResNet-18 under stratified group
$k$-fold cross validation, at patient or case level where the release gives
an identifier. For BUS-BRA we also reproduce the paper's own \textbf{ResNet-18 vs.\
ResNet-50} comparison, on its official fold assignment. Neither BUSI nor
BrEaST-Lesions USG reports its own classifier benchmark, so we add an
\textbf{EfficientNet-B0} comparison there instead, the backbone most used
in later work on these releases. We report accuracy, sensitivity,
specificity, precision, F1, and AUC for every run, and compare each result
to the source paper's own numbers where one exists. Beyond aggregate
accuracy, we break every model down by \textbf{false-positive vs.\
false-negative bias} and add a \textbf{Grad-CAM qualitative analysis} that
checks whether each model attends to the lesion or to background/framing
cues, directly probing a preprocessing concern raised by the aggregate
results. We also report \textbf{preliminary smoke-test results} on two
newly wired-in mammography sources, CDD-CESM \citep{khaled2022cddcesm} and
CMMD \citep{cui2021cmmd}, which confirm the pipeline end to end but are not
yet tuned reproductions, plus a third, BCS-DBT \citep{buda2021bcsdbt},
whose 8-image validation split makes it a pipeline check only. We report two \textbf{combined 3-branch fusion runs} that add I-SPY2
DCE-MRI \citep{fridman2025breastdcedl} as a malignant-only MRI source, and
a \textbf{BiomedCLIP} \citep{zhang2023biomedclip} fine-tuning baseline over
all ten sources. In both, the headline accuracy is inflated by the
malignant-only I-SPY2 data, so we also give per-modality and
per-source breakdowns. Finally, we LoRA fine-tune
\textbf{MedGemma-4B-it} \citep{sellergren2025medgemma}, a generative
vision-language model, on the same manifest, and run \textbf{Grad-CAM on
both VLMs' misclassified examples} using a ViT-adapted Grad-CAM we add to
\texttt{utils/gradcam.py}. The two VLMs fail on the same specific images
in their three smallest mammography sources, and MedGemma's Grad-CAM maps
show a grid artifact not present in BiomedCLIP's, which we read as a
methodological finding about Grad-CAM on quantized generative VLMs rather
than as reliable localization. We also extend Grad-CAM to every
remaining trained model that lacked it: BCS-DBT's single-modality
checkpoint, and -- for the first time in this report -- all five
\textbf{\texttt{FusionLateModel}} checkpoints, via a new
\textbf{\texttt{FusionBranchGradCAM}} that hooks all three branches at
once and reads out whichever one a given sample populates.
\end{abstract}

\section{Results}

\subsection{Training protocol}

Every backbone is ImageNet-pretrained. We train with SGD (momentum $0.9$,
learning rate $10^{-3}$) and class-weighted cross-entropy, for a fixed 100
epochs with no early stopping. We keep the checkpoint with the lowest
validation loss. We compute metrics on a held-out test fold that never
appears in training or checkpoint selection. Reported values are mean $\pm$
standard deviation across five folds. BreastDM is the exception: it uses
the dataset's own official train/validation split, with no cross-validation.
EfficientNet-B0 runs use the same optimizer, loss, and epoch budget as the
ResNet runs. All inputs are $224\times224$ except BUSC, which is
$128\times128$ to match the PDF-UNet release's stated resolution.

\subsection{Preprocessing}
\label{sec:preprocessing}

Preprocessing depends on what annotation each release provides (Table
\ref{tab:preprocessing}). BUS-BRA, BUSI, and BrEaST-Lesions USG each ship a
per-image tumor mask, so they share one pipeline: grayscale, $3\times3$
median filter, mask-guided ROI crop with a 5\% margin, train-time flips and
rotation ($\pm10^\circ$) and scale-crop, resize, an optional 5th/95th-
percentile contrast stretch, then replicate to 3 channels. We apply the
contrast stretch here, matching \citet{gomezflores2024busbra}. BUSC and
mini-MIAS skip it; their transform does not implement it. mini-MIAS instead
crops a lesion-centered patch at $1.5\times$ the annotated radius, using
the coordinates in \texttt{Info.txt}. BUSC's public release has no mask and
no lesion coordinates, so we resize the full image with no crop.
BreastDM's \texttt{img9Se} arrays are already derived by the dataset's own
pipeline. We only rescale them to $[0,1]$ and bilinearly resize each of the
nine slice channels. Augmentation there is flip-only, with no rotation, to
avoid interpolating across slice boundaries.

\subsection{Per-class bias: false positives vs.\ false negatives}
\label{sec:bias}

Aggregate accuracy hides which direction a model errs in, which matters
clinically: a false positive (predicting malignant on a benign case) costs
an unnecessary follow-up, while a false negative (predicting benign on a
malignant case) is a missed cancer. Table~\ref{tab:bias} pools confusion
counts across all 5 test folds per model (BreastDM's single 117-sample
split) and reports the resulting false-positive rate ($1-\text{specificity}$),
false-negative rate ($1-\text{sensitivity}$), and a pooled bias verdict.

\textbf{mini-MIAS is the most clinically concerning model here}: it misses
just over half of actual malignant cases (FN rate \textbf{0.510}), and this
direction holds in 4 of its 5 folds, not a coin flip. Combined with its
already-flagged small-sample caveat, it should not be treated as usable for
screening in its current form. \textbf{BUSI leans false-negative
consistently}, 5/5 folds for ResNet-18: despite a respectable 0.884
aggregate accuracy, it misses malignant cases about twice as often as it
false-alarms on benign ones. \textbf{BreastDM leans false-positive
severely}, over-calling malignant on 46\% of benign cases while almost
never missing a real malignant case (FN rate 0.032) — the safer failure
direction clinically, but still a heavy false-alarm burden. BUSC's 2
pooled errors are too few to call a direction; the near-perfect result
still needs the saliency check below, not a bias read. BUS-BRA and
BrEaST-Lesions USG's pooled verdicts hide real fold-to-fold flips (BUS-BRA
ResNet-18 is FP-leaning in 3/5 folds and FN-leaning in 2; BrEaST ResNet-18
flips 3 times across its 5 folds), so treat those two as a weak aggregate
tendency, not a stable property of the model.

\section{Discussion: Is the Preprocessing Sufficient?}
\label{sec:preprocessing-discussion}

Section~\ref{sec:preprocessing} described two pipelines: a mask-guided ROI
crop with contrast stretching (BUS-BRA, BUSI, BrEaST-Lesions USG), and a
lighter pipeline with no crop or contrast stretch (BUSC, mini-MIAS, and
BreastDM's already-derived arrays). Tables~\ref{tab:busbra}--\ref{tab:resnet-only}
let us ask, per dataset, whether that choice was adequate or needs further
investigation.

\paragraph{Where preprocessing looks adequate.} BUS-BRA reproduces the
paper's own 5-fold numbers within about \textbf{0.03--0.04 accuracy and
AUC} (Table~\ref{tab:busbra}), using the mask-guided crop and contrast
stretch. We can already explain the remaining gap: our pipeline replicates
grayscale into 3 channels, while the paper encodes the mask into R/G/B
channels (Section~\ref{sec:limitations}). \textbf{The crop and
contrast-stretch steps are not the cause.} BUSI reaches the literature's
commonly cited \textbf{90--96\%} accuracy range with the same pipeline,
unchanged. BreastDM needs almost no preprocessing of its own, since the
release's \texttt{img9Se} arrays are already derived, and still lands
within \textbf{0.002 accuracy} of the paper's purpose-built fusion
architecture. In these three cases, more preprocessing effort means results
closer to the literature. \textbf{The numbers give no reason for further
preprocessing work here.}

\paragraph{Where preprocessing is a real suspect.} BUSC is the opposite
signal. It has the \textbf{least preprocessing} of any dataset here: no ROI
crop at all, since the release ships neither a mask nor lesion coordinates
(Table~\ref{tab:preprocessing}). It also has the \textbf{best result by a
wide margin}: \textbf{0.992--0.9997} across every metric and every fold
(Table~\ref{tab:resnet-only}). This is the pattern we would expect if the
model is keying on something other than the lesion, since there is no crop
step to exclude background context. A device-specific border, an
annotation artifact, or an acquisition setting could correlate with the
class label in this 250-image release. \textbf{More cross-validation will
not resolve this.} It needs a \textbf{Grad-CAM or saliency check} on the
trained model. If a lesion boundary can be recovered by other means, such
as manual annotation or a generic saliency-based ROI heuristic, a re-run
with an actual crop would show whether accuracy drops toward the other
ultrasound datasets' range. Until that check happens, \textbf{treat BUSC's
number as unverified}, not as a working classifier.

\paragraph{Where the picture is genuinely unclear.} mini-MIAS and
BrEaST-Lesions USG both crop before classification: a radius-based lesion
patch for mini-MIAS, a mask-guided ROI for BrEaST. Both still produce
\textbf{weak or unstable results}. mini-MIAS gets \textbf{$0.599 \pm 0.096$}
accuracy; BrEaST's sensitivity std reaches \textbf{0.201}. Both are also
the \textbf{two smallest datasets} by patient or case count, so we cannot
yet tell whether preprocessing (crop margin, patch size, missing contrast
stretch) or sample size causes the weak results. Two follow-ups would
separate these causes. First, apply the BUS-BRA-style contrast stretch to
mini-MIAS, currently skipped (Table \ref{tab:preprocessing}); this is a
one-line change in \texttt{MiasTransform}. Second, for mini-MIAS, compare
the current lesion-patch framing against a full-breast-image baseline.
\citet{suckling1994mias} does not require the patch framing, and
\texttt{dataset\_reproduction\_plan} lists it as an open design choice.
Until we run one of these, \textbf{we cannot rule preprocessing in or out
as the bottleneck here}.

does not prove, the concern raised above: the model may be partly using
broad image texture or device-specific framing rather than a localized
lesion signal. \textbf{Treat BUSC's 0.992 accuracy as unverified}, not as a
working classifier, until lesion boundaries can be recovered by another
means and a crop re-run shows whether accuracy drops toward the other
ultrasound datasets' range.

representative BUSI false positive and a BreastDM false positive both show

background artifact: \textbf{the crop preprocessing appears to be doing
its job for these four datasets.}

\textbf{mini-MIAS fails diffusely too, but differently from BUSC.} Its
failures show a blocky, grid-like activation pattern with no clear center,
plausibly an artifact of Grad-CAM's coarse spatial resolution on an
already-small lesion-patch crop rather than a meaningful shortcut signal.

miss rate in Table~\ref{tab:bias}: mini-MIAS's failures are not
interpretable either way, so Grad-CAM cannot confirm or rule out a
preprocessing cause here.

Net read: extending the single-example BUSC check to every trained model
\textbf{strengthens rather than weakens} the preprocessing discussion's
BUSC concern, and adds a new, independent flag for mini-MIAS. BUS-BRA,
BUSI, and BreastDM's failures look like ordinary hard-case mistakes, not a
preprocessing or shortcut-learning problem.

provably attending to the crop boundary between real tissue and padded

scale (2/2 pooled false positives reach the image border), reinforcing that
its 0.992 accuracy should stay unverified pending a re-crop. The sweep also
extends this scrutiny to the two datasets added in

already proposed for BUSC and mini-MIAS: recover or approximate a

though with only 8 validation images and 2 errors total, this is far too
little evidence to call BCS-DBT's pipeline clean, only to say its errors
don't show the same border-artifact signature as the other three
mammography sources.

Beyond the border-artifact concerns above, BUS-BRA, BUSI, and
BrEaST-Lesions USG's worst false positives
mostly stay lesion-plausible (with one exception, BrEaST's
EfficientNet-B0 run, which diffuses across tissue boundaries instead),
consistent with Section~\ref{sec:gradcam}'s read that their mask-guided
crop is doing its job.

\section{Mixed-Backbone Multimodal Fusion}
\label{sec:mixed-fusion}

All results so far are single-modality baselines. A separate follow-up
study, \texttt{docs/mixed\_backbone\_results.md}, trains the repository's
3-branch late-fusion model instead: \texttt{FusionLateModel}, with
mammography from mini-MIAS, ultrasound from BUS-BRA, BUSI, BUSC, and
BrEaST-Lesions USG, and MRI from BreastDM. It varies the ultrasound
branch's backbone and initialization to separate three effects an earlier
single run had confounded: ResNet-18 vs.\ ResNet-50, warm-starting from a
converged single-dataset checkpoint, and RadImageNet vs.\ ImageNet
pretraining. Mammography and MRI stay fixed across all three runs:
ResNet-18, warm-started from \texttt{runs/run-1}'s single-dataset
checkpoints. Only the ultrasound branch varies. All four runs share one
combined validation set of 427 samples (10 mammography, 300 ultrasound, 117
MRI) and use the default recipe, not \texttt{paper-match}: Adam, a 10-epoch
budget, early-stopping patience 5. This is not the SGD/weighted-CE protocol
used for the single-dataset results above.

Holding the two warm-started branches fixed and varying only the ultrasound
branch isolates three effects:

\begin{enumerate}
  \item \textbf{Warm-starting from a converged single-dataset model is the
    main effect.} Both warm-started runs (A: \textbf{0.421}, B:
    \textbf{0.395}) beat both non-warm-started runs (baseline:
    \textbf{0.467}, C: \textbf{0.428}) by a similar margin. Backbone and
    pretraining source do not change this.
  \item \textbf{RadImageNet beats ImageNet when training in-loop.}
    Comparing the two non-warm-started runs directly, baseline (ImageNet)
    vs.\ Run C (RadImageNet), both trained from scratch inside the fusion
    loop, RadImageNet initialization helps. This is the \textbf{clearest
    evidence so far} that RadImageNet adds value on its own.
  \item \textbf{ResNet-18 edges out ResNet-50 under the same warm-start
    treatment} (Run B \textbf{0.395} vs.\ Run A \textbf{0.421}). This
    matches the BUS-BRA paper's own finding (Table~\ref{tab:busbra}) and
    this report's single-mode BUS-BRA 5-fold results. \textbf{The
    ResNet-50 swap is not the source of improvement} over the baseline.
    Under matched warm-start treatment, it is a slight drag compared to
    ResNet-18.
\end{enumerate}

\paragraph{Caveats specific to this study.} The mammography branch's
per-epoch accuracy comes from only \textbf{10 validation samples}. Read it
as noise in every run. The ultrasound warm-start (Runs A/B) uses only
\textbf{one of the five} BUS-BRA cross-validation folds, fold 1, not an
aggregate. None of these four runs use the \texttt{paper-match} recipe from
Section~\ref{sec:preprocessing-discussion} onward, so they are \textbf{not
as rigorous} as this report's single-dataset numbers. Early stopping also
cuts the three runs at different points: 9, 10, and 7 epochs. Run B hit the
full 10-epoch budget still improving, so its true optimum may be higher.
\textbf{This is a fusion-mechanism ablation, not a standalone benchmark
result.}

\section{Preliminary Extensions: CDD-CESM, CMMD, and BCS-DBT Mammography}
\label{sec:new-datasets}

Two additional mammography sources, CDD-CESM \citep{khaled2022cddcesm} and
CMMD \citep{cui2021cmmd}, were wired into \texttt{training/train.py} as
\texttt{--dataset cdd\_cesm} and \texttt{--dataset cmmd} after the
six-dataset results above. Both required a nontrivial data path the other
mammography source (mini-MIAS) does not: CDD-CESM needed joining images
against a separate TCIA annotation spreadsheet by patient/side/view, and
CMMD needed DICOM-to-array conversion (\texttt{pydicom} plus VOI-LUT
windowing) joined against a separate clinical-data spreadsheet by
\texttt{(PatientID, LeftRight)}, since CMMD's DICOM headers alone carry no
diagnosis. Both use a patient-level train/validation split and
\texttt{MiasTransform} for grayscale resize, matching the mini-MIAS
baseline above. \textbf{These are single-split smoke runs, not
cross-validated reproductions} — no augmentation search, hyperparameter
sweep, or (for CDD-CESM) the paper's own CM+DM two-input recipe. Table
\ref{tab:new-datasets} reports them for completeness; do not compare them
directly to the 5-fold numbers in Tables~\ref{tab:busbra}--\ref{tab:resnet-only}.

\textbf{CDD-CESM} (326 patients; 529 train / 57 validation images,

image rather than treating it as a second input. Specificity is weak
(\textbf{0.370}) and train/val loss diverged quickly, consistent with
overfitting on roughly 590 total labeled images with no augmentation or
class weighting yet.

\textbf{CMMD} (3,370 train / 368 validation images, patient-level split,
seed 42) demonstrates the class-imbalance failure mode directly: with
unweighted cross-entropy the model \textbf{collapses to predicting
malignant for almost everything} (specificity \textbf{0.010}), exploiting
the dataset's roughly 70/30 malignant/benign split — its 0.739 accuracy is
barely above the majority-class baseline and should not be read as a
result. Class-weighted cross-entropy (now applied unconditionally for

point but still not a tuned reproduction.

tomosynthesis screening collection, was added as a third DICOM-derived
mammography source. Its DICOM headers carry no diagnosis, so labels come
from TCIA's separate validation-partition label, file-path, and
bounding-box CSVs; the train-partition labels are not public. The 280
locally downloaded patients are exactly that validation partition. We keep
only biopsy-confirmed \textbf{Benign} and \textbf{Cancer} view-rows (38 and
37), dropping Normal and Actionable. Every one of these 75 view-rows has a
lesion box, so each sample is a box-cropped 2D slice (6\,px margin, VOI-LUT
and min-max normalized) from the annotated frame of the tomosynthesis
volume, not a full 3D volume. A patient-level split gives \textbf{67
training / 8 validation} images (40 patients, none with both labels); the
validation split is 2 benign / 6 malignant. The run used the default
recipe for 10 epochs; the checkpoint with the lowest validation loss is
epoch 8. At that checkpoint the model gets both benign cases right and
misses 2 of 6 malignant ones (accuracy $0.750$). Per-epoch validation
accuracy moved between $0.50$ and $0.875$, a swing of three predictions.
\textbf{With 8 validation images, the perfect specificity and AUC in
Table~\ref{tab:new-datasets} are not estimates of anything}; this run only
confirms that the label join and DICOM slice extraction work.

All three runs confirm their respective pipelines are correct end to end. The
next steps before either is comparable to
Tables~\ref{tab:busbra}--\ref{tab:resnet-only} are the same as elsewhere in
this report: cross-validation, an augmentation/hyperparameter search, and,
for CMMD, breast-level multi-view aggregation rather than per-image
predictions. BCS-DBT additionally needs more labeled data than the public
validation partition provides before any of its numbers can be trusted.

\section{Combined Fusion with I-SPY2 MRI and BCS-DBT}
\label{sec:combined-ispy2}

Section~\ref{sec:mixed-fusion}'s fusion runs use a 427-sample combined
validation set. Two later \texttt{--dataset combined} runs add two new
sources, so their validation set is different and their numbers are
\textbf{not comparable} to Table~\ref{tab:mixed-fusion}.

\paragraph{I-SPY2 as a malignant-only MRI source.} The BreastDCEDL release
\citep{fridman2025breastdcedl} supplies I-SPY2 DCE-MRI volumes with tumor
masks for 982 patients. I-SPY2 is a neoadjuvant-chemotherapy trial cohort,
so every case is biopsy-confirmed malignant and there is no benign class.
We convert each tumor-bearing axial slice into a BreastDM-style
\texttt{img9Se} patch: crop to the mask's bounding box with a 6\,px margin,
resample to 9 DCE phases, and normalize per patient to uint8 by
1st/99th-percentile clipping of the pre-contrast volume. A patient-level
90/10 split is used. The MRI branch loads these patches as a second source
next to BreastDM. The sampler balances branch, then source within branch,
then class within source, so an all-malignant source adds malignant
examples without needing a benign counterpart. \textbf{This is data
augmentation for the MRI branch, not a new benchmark}; no published I-SPY2
benign-vs-malignant result exists to reproduce.

\paragraph{Runs.} Both runs use three ImageNet-pretrained ResNet-18
branches with no warm-start, the default Adam recipe, batch size 8, a
10-epoch budget, and early-stopping patience 5. Run D adds I-SPY2 (37{,}510
training / 4{,}132 validation samples). Run E then also adds BCS-DBT as a
second mammography source next to mini-MIAS (4{,}140 validation samples).
Table~\ref{tab:combined-ispy2} reports the checkpoint with the lowest
validation loss.

\texttt{FusionLateModel} checkpoints: the
mixed-backbone study's Baseline/A/B/C (Section~\ref{sec:mixed-fusion}) and
Run E (Section~\ref{sec:combined-ispy2}, Run D's checkpoint was overwritten
so is not recoverable). Since a fused forward pass always runs all three
backbones even on a zero-filled absent branch, we added
\texttt{FusionBranchGradCAM}, which hooks all three branches' last conv
layer at once and reads out whichever branch a given sample actually
populates.

Baseline/A/B/C share the original 427-sample validation set (mini-MIAS +
BUS-BRA/BUSI/BUSC/BrEaST + BreastDM, no I-SPY2/BCS-DBT, since those sources
did not exist when those checkpoints were trained); Run E uses the current
4{,}140-sample set. Rebuilding the old composition by hand, rather than
calling \nolinkurl{training/train.py}'s own \texttt{\_build\_combined\_sources}
(which now includes I-SPY2/BCS-DBT unconditionally whenever those
directories exist locally), was necessary to avoid silently evaluating the
older checkpoints against a validation set they were never trained for; we
verified the reconstructed counts match exactly (427 and 4{,}140) before
running Grad-CAM. Up to 5 wrong cases per source per checkpoint were saved,
132 heatmaps total across all five checkpoints and every branch, to
\nolinkurl{docs/gradcam_fusion/} with a manifest at
\nolinkurl{docs/gradcam_fusion_manifest.json}.

\textbf{The mammography branch's artifact-proneness carries over from the
single-modality model into the fusion model, but not uniformly.} Of
Baseline's 3 mini-MIAS wrong cases, one shows the corner/boundary pattern in
Figure~\ref{fig:gradcam-fusion}; the other two show ordinary compact,
roughly central hotspots instead -- a mix, not a repeat of the single-model
sweep's single clean example. mini-MIAS's small size (10 validation images)
means this mix is itself low-confidence evidence either way. In Run E,
BCS-DBT's mammography-branch errors (4 of them, Table~\ref{tab:combined-ispy2}'s
18-sample mammography pool) show a mix of a compact bottom-of-frame hotspot
and a horizontal-striping artifact plausibly from tomosynthesis
reconstruction or {JPEG2000} compression, not the same background-boundary
pattern as mini-MIAS or the single-modality {CMMD} finding.

\textbf{The ultrasound and {MRI} branches are consistently lesion-plausible
across every checkpoint and backbone}, matching their single-modality
counterparts: BUS-BRA/BUSI/BrEaST-Lesions {USG} wrong cases are compact
hotspots in all four mixed-backbone runs (ResNet-18 and ResNet-50 alike, so
this is not a backbone-specific effect), and both {MRI} sources (BreastDM in
all five checkpoints, {I-SPY2} in Run E) show the same pattern on their
errors. This is the same read as the single-modality Grad-CAM sections above,
now shown to hold inside the fusion model too, not just when each backbone
is trained alone.

\textbf{BUSC reached 0 wrong cases in two of the five checkpoints} (Run B
and Run E) -- a perfect 25/25 ultrasound-branch score with nothing left to
Grad-CAM. This is the same unresolved concern as BUSC's single-modality
0.992 accuracy (Section~\ref{sec:gradcam}): a fusion-model Grad-CAM check
cannot confirm or rule out a shortcut when there is no error to inspect, so
BUSC's perfect scores stay unverified pending the same re-crop fix proposed
throughout this report, now also for the fusion setting.

\section{Foundation-Model Baseline: BiomedCLIP}
\label{sec:biomedclip}

As a first vision-language-model comparison, we fine-tuned the image tower
of BiomedCLIP \citep{zhang2023biomedclip} (a ViT-B/16)\footnote{Hugging
Face checkpoint
\nolinkurl{microsoft/BiomedCLIP-PubMedBERT_256-vit_base_patch16_224}.}
with a new linear head as a flat benign-vs-malignant classifier
(\nolinkurl{scripts/finetune_biomedclip.py}). Unlike \texttt{FusionLateModel},
this is a single-branch model on ordinary 3-channel $224\times224$ input.
All ten local sources go into one manifest and reuse each dataset's own
patient-safe split from \texttt{training/train.py}: mini-MIAS, CDD-CESM,
CMMD, and BCS-DBT (mammography); BUS-BRA, BUSI, BUSC, and BrEaST-Lesions
USG (ultrasound); BreastDM and I-SPY2 (MRI). MRI patches are reduced from 9
DCE phases to one representative phase (index 4) copied to three channels.
That gives 41{,}476 training and 4{,}565 validation images, 80.7\% of the
training rows from I-SPY2. Training samples are drawn with the same
modality, then source, then class balancing as the fusion runs. Training
used AdamW with learning rate $10^{-5}$ for the encoder and $10^{-3}$ for
the head, batch size 32, up to 20 epochs, and patience 5.

Validation loss was lowest after epoch 1 ($0.144$) and rose every epoch
after that while training accuracy reached $0.994$, so training stopped
after 6 epochs and the reported checkpoint is epoch 1.
Table~\ref{tab:biomedclip} breaks down its validation accuracy by source.

\section{Generative Vision-Language Model: {MedGemma} {LoRA}}
\label{sec:medgemma}

As a second comparison, we LoRA fine-tuned MedGemma-4B-it
\citep{sellergren2025medgemma}, a Gemma~3-based generative
image-text-to-text model with a SigLIP vision tower, pretrained on
de-identified medical images including radiology. MedGemma and MedSigLIP
are gated on Hugging Face; the account used here was granted access on
2026-10-06 (previously confirmed denied via the Hub API). Unlike
BiomedCLIP, MedGemma is not a classifier: each example is one chat turn,
the same image plus a fixed prompt (``Is this breast imaging finding
benign or malignant? Answer with one word...''), scored against the
model's own chat template, with the loss masked to only the one-word
answer tokens (5--6 tokens, including every one of the 256 image soft
tokens). We loaded the base model 4-bit (NF4), froze the vision tower
entirely, and LoRA-adapted only the language model's attention/MLP
projections ($r{=}16$, $\alpha{=}32$, 29.8M of 4.33B parameters trainable),
reusing \nolinkurl{scripts/finetune_biomedclip.py}'s manifest and the same
modality/source/class-balanced sampling. Training used AdamW at $2\times
10^{-4}$, gradient accumulation 8 (effective batch 8), 250 steps (2{,}000
training examples seen, 4.8\% of the 41{,}476-row manifest)
\footnote{The vision tower lives at \texttt{model.model.vision\_tower}
(\texttt{Gemma3ForConditionalGeneration} wraps an inner model), and this
machine's missing \texttt{python3-dev} broke PyTorch's {JIT}-compiled
{Triton} override of \texttt{aten::bmm} used by Gemma~3's rotary
embeddings; both are recorded in
\nolinkurl{docs/breast\_imaging\_vlms.md} in case they recur.}.

Training loss fell smoothly and monotonically throughout (0.153 at step
110 to 0.093 at step 250); as with BiomedCLIP, that alone does not imply
better generalization. Evaluation is unbatched greedy generation
($\sim$1.7\,s/sample on this {GPU}), so the final report below caps each
source at 40 samples (mini-MIAS and {BCS-DBT} have fewer than 40
validation rows total, so those two are their complete validation sets;
every other row is a 40-sample subset of a larger pool) rather than the
full 4{,}565-row validation set BiomedCLIP's table uses.

\subsection{{Grad-CAM} on the two {VLMs}' wrong cases}
\label{sec:gradcam-vlm}

Section~\ref{sec:gradcam}'s \texttt{GradCAM} class hooks a {CNN}'s last
spatial feature map, shape $[B,C,H,W]$; a {Vision} {Transformer} block
instead outputs a token sequence $[B,N,D]$. We added \texttt{ViTGradCAM}
to \texttt{utils/gradcam.py}, which drops any prefix/{CLS} tokens, reshapes
the remaining $N$ patch tokens into a spatial grid, and applies the same
gradient-weighted channel sum. For {BiomedCLIP} this hooks the {ViT-B/16}
trunk's last block ($14\times14$ patches, one {CLS} token dropped); for
MedGemma it hooks the {SigLIP} vision tower's last encoder layer
($64\times64$ patches, no prefix token), scoring the generated answer
token's logit instead of a classifier logit, with the loss masked exactly
as in training. \texttt{scripts/gradcam\_vlm\_failures.py} runs both
sweeps and saves every example to \texttt{docs/gradcam\_vlm/}.

\textbf{Both models fail on the same specific images in the three
smallest, hardest mammography sources.} Of {BiomedCLIP}'s wrong cases,
100\% of its {CMMD} (2/2 overlapping), {BCS-DBT} (2/2), and mini-MIAS
(1/1) mistakes are also among MedGemma's mistakes on those sources --
different architectures, different training recipes, same specific
images. This reads as evidence that these are genuinely hard or
borderline-labeled cases at the dataset level, not an idiosyncrasy of
either model. BreastDM is the exception: the two models' {MRI} mistakes
do not overlap at all, consistent with MedGemma's regression there being
a distinct new failure mode rather than the same hard cases scaled up.

whatever MedGemma's underlying classification behavior turns out to be.

\section{Limitations}
\label{sec:limitations}

\begin{itemize}
  \item \textbf{Split granularity.} BUS-BRA, BrEaST-Lesions USG, and
    mini-MIAS use patient- or case-level folds, via \texttt{K5B},
    \texttt{CaseID}, and \texttt{patient}. BUSI and BUSC have no patient
    identifier in their public release, so we split them at the image
    level. A fold boundary can still place near-duplicate crops of the
    same lesion on both sides.
  \item \textbf{BUS-BRA input encoding.} Our pipeline repeats the
    grayscale, contrast-stretched, mask-cropped image into three identical
    channels. The source paper instead encodes the lesion mask into
    separate R/G/B channels. This likely explains part of the remaining
    \textbf{0.03--0.04} accuracy/AUC gap in Table~\ref{tab:busbra}.
  \item \textbf{Single seed per fold.} All reported std is across the 5
    folds, not across repeated seeds within a fold. The paper's own
    reported std for BUS-BRA is also a single-seed, 5-fold figure.
  \item \textbf{Dataset scale.} mini-MIAS (about 130 lesion patches total)
    and BrEaST-Lesions USG (256 cases) are small enough that 5-fold CV
    still leaves high per-fold variance. Read results there as
    directional.
  \item \textbf{CDD-CESM and CMMD (Section~\ref{sec:new-datasets}) are
    single-split smoke tests, not cross-validated reproductions.} No
    augmentation search, hyperparameter sweep, or class-weighting tuning
    has been done for CDD-CESM, and CMMD's per-image predictions have not
    yet been aggregated to breast level. Neither should be compared
    directly to the 5-fold numbers elsewhere in this report.
  \item \textbf{Malignant-only I-SPY2 inflates pooled accuracy}
    (Sections~\ref{sec:combined-ispy2} and~\ref{sec:biomedclip}). It
    makes up most of the MRI and overall validation data in the combined
    and BiomedCLIP runs, so their MRI and overall accuracies mostly
    measure how often the model predicts malignant. Only the per-source
    numbers, and BreastDM evaluated on its own, measure discrimination.
  \item \textbf{BCS-DBT has 8 validation images} because only the public
    validation partition is labeled. Its single-mode metrics and its
    contribution to the combined mammography branch are not reliable.
  \item \textbf{Grad-CAM is a qualitative, not quantitative, check}
    (Sections~\ref{sec:gradcam},~\ref{sec:gradcam-fusion},
    and~\ref{sec:gradcam-vlm}). It is read on a handful of examples per
    model (up to 5 per source per fusion checkpoint in
    Section~\ref{sec:gradcam-fusion}) and cannot itself confirm or rule
    out shortcut learning; it motivates, rather than replaces, the
    re-crop experiment proposed for BUSC in
    Section~\ref{sec:preprocessing-discussion}. BUSC's two fusion
    checkpoints with a perfect ultrasound-branch score have no errors to
    inspect at all, so Grad-CAM says nothing about them either way.
  \item \textbf{MedGemma's results (Section~\ref{sec:medgemma}) are a
    single 250-step run, not a tuned comparison.} Its final per-source
    accuracies are mostly 40-sample subsets measured against BiomedCLIP's
    full validation sets, so only the mini-MIAS and BCS-DBT rows (equal,
    full $n$ for both models) are directly comparable; the apparent
    BreastDM regression should be re-measured on the full validation
    split before being treated as established. Its Grad-CAM maps also
    show a periodic grid artifact not present in BiomedCLIP's, so they
    should not be read for localization at all, only as a methodological
    finding about Grad-CAM on quantized generative VLMs.
\end{itemize}
